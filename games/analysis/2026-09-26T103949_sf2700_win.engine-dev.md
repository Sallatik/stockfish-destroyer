## Diagnosis — where the 157 plies went
**Stall: moves 35-61 / plies 69-121, about a third of the game.** 35.d7 creates a passed pawn on the 7th rank; White then shuffles rook and bishop for 26 moves (Ra7-Ra4-Rc4-Ba4-Rc1…, Bf1-Bb5-Bc6-Be4-Bc6) with no plan to dislodge Black's king/knight blockade, and the pawn is simply captured for free on move 61 (…Nxd7). Aside from the one flagged blunder, our own moves in this window still leak ~100-150cp cumulatively in sub-30cp dribs (Bc6 -29, h4 -18, Ra4 -8, Bb5 -8…) — no single error, just no technique.
**Root cause is an eval blind spot, not depth or draw-avoidance.** `eval::evaluate` (engine/src/eval.rs) is pure material+PST; PeSTO's EG_PAWN table already rewards a pawn *sitting* on the 7th (178cp), so nothing in the eval rewards actually spending moves to escort/promote it — a king walk or cutting off the enemy king scores the same as an aimless shuffle. Proof it's eval, not search depth: the same engine converted the *second* passer (kingside g-pawn) cleanly in 10 moves (65→75) once the plan was static-eval-visible. `is_repetition`/`draw_score` (main.rs:134-151, tested) behaved correctly throughout — this is not 50-move/repetition shuffling. The position only actually got won because Black (Elo-limited SF) blundered twice more in this stretch (ply78 f6 -46, ply80 Kd6 -185); against cleaner play this technique gap is a draw/loss risk, not just a slow win.
