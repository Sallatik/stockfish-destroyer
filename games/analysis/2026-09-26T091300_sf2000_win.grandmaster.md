# Grandmaster review — 2026-09-26T091300_sf2000_win

**Verdict**: Win earned mostly by conversion, not by outplaying a well-behaved opponent. SF2000 lost the opening tempo battle (4...Qb6, 9...Qa5-Qd8 shuffled the queen three times before castling), letting us build a free +20/+30cp space edge. The decisive moment was tactical, not positional: SF missed that recapturing wrong on d5 walked into a knight fork, and then blundered again by recapturing with the queen instead of the pawn. Our engine's positional play was accurate enough to never blunder (0 moves ≥100cp) but was consistently second-best (many 15-60cp "drop" moves) — it just was never punished for it.

## Recurring weaknesses
Not tactical blindness (no ≥100cp own mistakes) — the issue is a steady trickle of 15-60cp positional imprecision through the middlegame, most visible in plies 25-39, i.e. once the position gets messy with multiple minor-piece trades pending. The engine also shows no urgency to punish an uncastled king with tempo (kept maneuvering Bd4/Nbd2/Ne5 instead of pushing c4/e4 while black's king sat on e8 for 10 plies) — it just waits for material to fall rather than actively exploiting a development lead.

## Was the win earned / what a 2200 resists
Half-earned. The opening edge came from opponent weakness (queen shuffling), not engine insight. The finish (Nf6+/Bxf6/passed-pawn mate) was clean and best-move-accurate once the material appeared. A 2200 opponent castles early, never allows the tempo-losing queen tour, and simply recaptures correctly at moves 20/21 — the position stays roughly balanced (+50 to +80cp, not a forced tactic), and this game is a longer grind or a draw with the current level of positional play.
