# Destroyer 0.7.0 (white) vs SF2500 — win by mate, 43 plies (shortest of the four)

**Verdict:** Earned, and a good template for short games. Black never castled: 13...e6 (-181cp, best O-O) and 14...Bf8 (-240cp, best O-O again) both declined a free king-safety move. 17.Nd5! then hit the c7-queen with check-to-come, and Black's decisive error was 18...Bxd3 (-479cp, best Qd6) — grabbing the loose knight on d3 instead of moving the queen, letting 19.Nxc7+ simply win the queen with check. Our central pressure (10.Ne5, 13.Bf4, 17.Nd5) is exactly what punishes an uncastled king, and we found the finish (Nxc7+/Rxe6+/Re8#) without hesitation.

**Our lapse — 14.Nd3 (-182cp, best Nxf7):** confirmed with Stockfish depth 20 (+611 for Nxf7 vs the game's actual +388 after Nd3): Nxf7 keeps maximal pressure on a king still stuck on e8, while Nd3 is a quiet retreat that gives Black a free tempo. It didn't matter here — Black immediately erred worse (14...Bf8, then 18...Bxd3) — but it's the same species of mistake as choosing a quiet move when a forcing one keeps an opponent's king trapped in the center.

**Shorten the game:** it's already short (43 plies); the only miss was not making it more lopsided sooner by keeping the king-hunt going at move 14 instead of retreating.

When the opponent's king hasn't castled and a forcing continuation is available (capture/check that keeps it stuck in the center), prefer it over a quiet retreat even if material looks similar — Nxf7 vs Nd3 here is a clean tuning example for whatever move-ordering/eval term tracks "opponent king still in center past move ~12."
