# Grandmaster Analysis — Destroyer 0.2.0 (White) vs Stockfish 2000, 0-1 (mate, 28 moves)

## Verdict
White lost because it never developed a single minor piece for eight moves, could
therefore never castle, and eventually walked its king into an open kingside it had
itself ripped open (g4/gxh5). Material stayed roughly level until the very end; the
game was decided by development tempo and king safety, not tactics — until a single
losing blunder (23.Nd2) let a mating net close immediately. This is a textbook
material-only-eval loss: the engine had no concept of piece activity, king shelter,
or attack detection.

## Critical moments

**1. Moves 1–8 (ply 1–15), start FEN.** Engine played a3, b3, c3, d3, e3, f3, g3, h3
in order — every first move was a first/second-rank pawn push, zero pieces developed.
Eval drifted from 0 to -221 purely from this. A 2830-level player plays 1.e4/1.d4/1.Nf3
and completes development by move 6-7. The literal file-order pattern (a→h) is a
symptom of a flat material eval: every quiet non-capture move scores identically, so
move selection is effectively an artifact of move-generation order, not chess judgment.

**2. Move 10, ply 19, `exd4` (-117cp).** FEN
`rnbq1rk1/1p2bppp/5n2/p1p1p3/P2p4/1PPPPPPP/8/RNBQKBNR w KQ - 0 10`. Best was Ne2 —
finally developing the last idle knight. Instead the engine chose a central capture
that opens lines while still down a full piece's worth of tempo and with Bc1/Nb1/Ng1
all on their home squares. Pattern: the engine treats "a capture is available" as
sufficient reason to play it, regardless of development debt.

**3. Move 14, ply 27, `Bc3` (-151cp).** FEN
`rn1q1rk1/1p2bppp/8/p1P2b2/Pn6/1P1P1PPP/1B6/RN1QKBNR w KQ - 3 14`. Best was Na3,
developing the queenside knight (which never moves until move 23, straight into a
blunder). Bc3 retreats a piece already developed instead of bringing out an idle one.
By this point White has moved a bishop three times and a knight zero times — classic
"one piece hog" instead of completing the full set.

**4. Moves 17-22 (ply 33-44), king-safety collapse.** White plays g4 (ply33) and later
recaptures `gxh5` (-424cp, ply41) and `a5` (-332cp, ply43) while Black's queen (h4→g3),
bishop (d4) and knight (c5) are actively swarming the kingside. g4/gxh5 rips open the
g-file directly in front of White's own king, which had already lost castling rights
by walking Kf1 (ply37) because f1/g1 were still blocked by undeveloped pieces. a5 is a
do-nothing queenside push while mate threats are brewing two files away. The engine
shows no awareness that its own pawn moves are demolishing its king's shelter, and no
threat-detection to redirect play toward defense (e.g. f4, Nd2 consolidating).

**5. Move 23, ply 45, `Nd2` (-9341cp, allows forced mate).** FEN
`r3r1k1/1p3pp1/4b3/P1n4P/1p1b3q/1P1P1P1P/R3B2R/1N1Q1KN1 w - - 0 23`. Best was f4. With
5s/move and depth 18 the engine still walked into a forced mate in a handful of moves —
the single largest eval swing of the game. Given Bd4+Nc5+Qg3 already aimed at the king
and only Ng1/Rh2/Be2 defending, this was a detectable mating attack, not a deep tactic.

## Recurring weaknesses
- No development incentive at all: minor pieces (especially both knights) sit on
  their home squares for 20+ plies while pawns are pushed and traded.
- Never castles — not by choice, but because development neglect leaves f1/g1 (and
  the corresponding queenside squares) blocked until it's too late and rights are lost.
- Opens lines toward its own king while behind in development and under attack
  (g4/gxh5), instead of consolidating.
- Queen (d1) never leaves its home square for the entire 28-move game — total absence
  of a coordinated attack/defense plan.
- Fails to detect a forming forced-mate attack even at 5s/depth-18, producing one
  catastrophic blunder rather than a slow decline.

This game strongly confirms the JOURNAL priority of **tapered PSTs (#1)** — the
alphabetical pawn-shuffle opening and the "develop one piece, ignore the rest"
pattern are direct, textbook symptoms of having no piece-square evaluation at all —
and of **king safety (#8)**, specifically the un-castled-king exposure pattern seen
here, which is currently completely unweighted.

## Ranked action items (new, beyond the queued plan)
1. **Add an explicit "king still in center" penalty distinct from endgame mop-up**:
   penalize own pawn moves/captures that open a file or diagonal adjacent to a king
   that hasn't castled and has pieces still blocking O-O/O-O-O (this game's engine
   played g4 then gxh5 in front of its own uncastled king while under attack — a
   pattern general king-safety/mop-up scoring won't catch if it's endgame-only).
2. **Add check/threat-aware search extensions** so multi-move mating nets are found
   at normal depth: the -9341cp blunder at move 23 happened despite 5s and depth 18,
   suggesting checks/forcing sequences near the king aren't being extended or that
   king danger isn't reducing pruning aggressiveness in that subtree.
3. **Add a cheap "development gate" heuristic as a stop-gap before full PSTs land**:
   discourage a 2nd/3rd/4th pawn move on a wing while a same-side minor piece is still
   on its home square — this directly targets the observed a3-b3-c3-...-h3 opening,
   which is otherwise possible even after PSTs are added if PST weights are small
   relative to search noise.
4. **Break ties in flat-eval quiet positions with a small mobility bonus** (legal-move
   count after the move) rather than move-generation order — explains the literal
   alphabetical a-file-to-h-file pawn-push pattern in the opening and will reduce
   directionless play in any future quiet position where eval terms tie.
5. **Track "piece hasn't moved by move N" as a debug/eval signal in self-play testing**
   (e.g. flag games where a knight or the queen never leaves its home square) to catch
   this class of bug quickly in future A/B gauntlets, before it costs an official game.
