# Grandmaster review — 2026-09-26T092447_sf2200_win (vs SF 2200)

## Verdict
We won a game we should have controlled comfortably but nearly threw away. Destroyer built a clean +2/+3 advantage out of the opening (SF blundered a knight around move 15-16), then twice weakened its own king for no reason during an open, queens-on middlegame, letting Black's queen generate checks that briefly swung the eval to -338. The win only stands because SF2200 answered the critical moment with the second-best move (e5 instead of Bd6) and never found a way back in.

## Critical moments (the g4 / Kf3 family)
- **Ply 35, g4 (played) vs Nxf5 (correct).** FEN `r3kb1r/1p1b2p1/p3pq2/3p1p2/3N4/P1N1P1P1/1PPQP1B1/2R2RK1 w kq - 1 18`. Up +217 with a clean extra pawn/structure edge, Destroyer instead pushed g3-g4, opening its own king's diagonal/second rank with queens still on the board and Black's queen already eyeing h4/h2. Nxf5 wins the pawn cleanly and keeps the kingside closed. A 2830-level player never voluntarily loosens the king's shelter when the alternative is "just take the free pawn." Eval instantly collapses from +217 to 0.
- **Ply 41, Kf3 (played) vs Kg1 (correct).** FEN `r3kb1r/1p1b2p1/p3p3/3p1P2/3N3q/P1N1P3/1PPQPKB1/2R2R2 w kq - 3 21`, under a queen check on h4. Kg1 tucks the king back to safety on the back rank, out of forks and discovered-check lines. Kf3 walks the king toward the center of an open board with Black's e-pawn and pieces still active — exactly the kind of "king in no-man's-land while queens are on" move a strong human avoids on reflex. The engine's own search rated this as equal (0) beforehand but the true score was -338: a real search blindspot, not just a missing heuristic, since the refutation (e5 followed by ...Bd6 keeping the bind) was well within normal tactical depth for a 2200+ engine.
- **How close this was:** after Kf3, SF was already winning material/initiative. It let it slip by choosing e5 (eval back to 0) instead of the stronger Bd6, which would have kept Black clearly better. Against SF2300+, this exact sequence is a probable loss, not a survived scare — the follow-up accuracy needed from Black was minimal.

## Recurring weakness
This is the same family as the SF2100 loss: the engine treats king safety as free once material/PST favor it, and it doesn't look far enough down forcing-check lines to notice that a "quiet" king move (Kf3) or pawn push (g4) hands the initiative back. Both blunders happened precisely when queens were on and the king had already been flushed from castling.

## Ranked action items (confirms/reorders the existing plan queue)
1. **King safety** — move this above killer/history in priority. Concretely: penalize king moves toward open central/kingside files while enemy queen is on board and material is not overwhelming (e.g. Kf3-type moves into the center), and penalize self-inflicted pawn shield damage (g3-g4 with king still behind it) unless it wins forced material.
2. **Check extensions + qsearch fixes** — the Kf3 blunder was a search failure (static eval said 0, true eval -338) inside an active check sequence; extend search on lines with repeated in-check moves so these 2-3 ply refutations (e5→...Bd6 bind) are seen before the move is played, not after.
3. **Search depth/time on "won" positions** — when already up decisive material (+2 to +3), the engine should be even more conservative about voluntarily exposing its own king rather than simplifying/consolidating; consider a search-time or eval bias for king-safety when ahead so it stops "helping" the opponent generate play.
