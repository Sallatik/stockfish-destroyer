# Grandmaster review — 2026-09-25T124442_sf2000_win (Destroyer 0.3.0 vs SF2000)
## Verdict
Gifted win, not earned. Opening/early play bled eval steadily (b4/Ba3/Nc3/Ra4-style
flank shuffling) until a real inaccuracy on move 16 handed Black a near-winning edge
(-113). Black then blundered far bigger the very next move and kept blundering
tactically through the rook ending (drops of 132, 142, 66, 126, ~9000cp mate-walk).
Endgame conversion from move 20 on was clean, but SF2000's Elo cap decided the game.

## Critical moments
- **16.axb5** (`r3k2r/1p2qpp1/5n1p/pp2p3/P1n1P3/2NQ1B2/2P2PPP/R4RK1 w kq - 0 16`):
  played axb5 (-20 -> -113); Nxb5 keeps central control, denies Nc4/Qb4 activity.
  The one moment a 2200+ opponent punishes outright instead of reciprocating.
- **16...Qb4**: Black had -113 (winning), played Qb4, dropping to +24 — a 137cp
  self-inflicted blunder (Rc8 correct). The real turning point, and it's Black's error.
- **21.b6** (drop 66, best Nd5): pushed the passer early instead of centralizing the
  knight first. Harmless here since Black kept blundering; vs SF2200 this
  push-because-PST-likes-it style cedes counterplay before the pawn queens.
- **38...Rxc2??**: Black hangs a rook into forced mate (-899 -> -9990). We mated
  cleanly once handed the material, but this win condition won't recur vs SF2200.
## Recurring weaknesses
Opening has no coherent plan, just PST-gradient following (b4/a4/Ba3/Ra4 —
disconnected flank pushes, Ba3 offside); cumulative opening eval loss over moves
1-16 rivals the one real blunder. Also: premature pawn pushes ahead of piece
activity in winning positions, and shallow calculation in knight-fork middlegames
(no flagged mistakes this game, but the axb5 lapse shows the ceiling).

## Ranked concrete action items
1. Bump **opening book** to top priority. Nearly every one of our first 9 moves loses
   10-50cp to a "reasonable but second-best" PST move. Cosmetic vs SF2000; against
   SF2200 it compounds into a lost middlegame — we got lucky this only produced one
   real inaccuracy (axb5).
2. The axb5-vs-Nxb5 miss in a knight-fork middlegame argues for prioritizing
   **TT + PVS/LMR search depth** ahead of quieter items — a calculation miss, not an
   eval miss, so deeper/faster search is the direct fix.
3. When implementing the queued passed-pawn bonus, gate pawn advances on "no piece
   move gains more activity" (e.g. don't push b5-b6 when Nd5 is stronger and the pawn
   isn't yet securable) rather than a flat rank-scaled bonus alone.
4. No change to king-safety/mop-up priority from this game — our king was never
   tested, so no signal either way.
