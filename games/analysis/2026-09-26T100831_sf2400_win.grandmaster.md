(-3.7→-5.1) — walking into 13.Nb6! forking queen and rook, so 14.Nxa8 wins the exchange for nothing. From move 14 Black was strategically dead; we mated in an unhurried but error-free mop-up. Our own play never crossed the mistake threshold, but drifted 6-33cp below best on six opening moves (3.Nf3, 4.Nc3, 5.d4, 7.Nd5, 9.Bc4, 11.Bb3) — under a pawn total, harmless only because Black then handed us the game outright.

**Critical moments:**
- 12...Qd5?? / 13...Qh5??: this is the whole game. Nothing we did induced it beyond ordinary development pressure — a 2400 shouldn't do this twice in a row.
- 25.Qd5+ (ours; best was Qxf5+, -1.06): at +11.6 we chose a spite-check over the cleaner capture that keeps simplifying toward mate. Free here (still +10.5, mated 5 moves later), but it's the same "check first, calculate later" instinct that costs real ground in a tight conversion.

**What SF2500+ punishes:** the opening drift, not the tactics. A 2500 doesn't hang a rook on moves 12-13; if the game had stayed level, six small concessions (worst: 3.Nf3 over Bb5+, -0.33) would have added up to a real, contestable middlegame instead of a free point.

**Ranked action items:**
1. Extend opening book past its current ~3-ply exit in offbeat 1...d5/2...g6 lines — the engine starts choosing its own (measurably weaker) moves exactly where book coverage runs out.
2. Add a move-selection tie-break, active once eval > +500: prefer the most forcing/simplifying legal move (captures, mate-shortening) over a bare check of similar eval (Qxf5+ over Qd5+), for faster, more reliable conversions.
