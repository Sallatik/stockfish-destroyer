## Recurring weaknesses
- **No concept of "safe square" beyond one ply.** King visits g1→h1→g1→h1→f2 repeatedly, always choosing the square that merely isn't in check *this* move, never one that stays safe next move too — pure horizon effect from a flat eval.
- **No instinct to trade queens or block with tempo when mated-down.** Every response to a check was a king step, never interposing the bishop/rook or offering a trade, even when material was already lost and simplification was the only practical try.
- **No pull toward known drawn endgame structures.** Once material settled near R+B vs Q, moves were still aimless (Bf1↔Be2 shuffles) rather than actively centralizing the king near the rook/bishop to build the fortress — the repetition draw was accidental, not steered.

## Ranked action items
1. **Add a "king exposed" penalty tied to open diagonals/files toward the king and loss of castling rights**, independent of the general king-safety/PST work already queued — specifically bias move ordering so that recaptures/pawn pushes that open a checking line are deprioritized versus alternatives that block it (this is what turned dxe4 into a strategic disaster the 148cp drop didn't capture).
2. **When in check or facing a forcing sequence of checks, give a search bonus to moves that block with a piece or offer a queen trade over king steps of equal immediate eval** — the engine never once tried to simplify against the mating attack.
3. **Detect low-material-imbalance "fortress" endgames (Q vs R+minor, opposite bishops) and reward king centralization/piece coordination toward them** when already materially worse, so survival becomes a plan instead of a lucky repetition.
4. **Log/flag "king revisits same square within N plies while eval is dropping"** as a self-diagnostic during self-play testing — it's a cheap signal that the king-walk pattern seen here (g1-h1-g1-h1-f2) is recurring and getting worse, not converging to safety.
