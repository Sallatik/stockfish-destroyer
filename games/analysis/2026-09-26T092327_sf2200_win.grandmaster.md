# Grandmaster review — 2026-09-26T092327_sf2200_win (Destroyer 0.4.0 vs SF 2200, 1-0, 117 plies)

## Verdict
Solid, earned positional edge out of a symmetrical English/Reti (Destroyer kept the more active minor pieces and a small space plus through move ~25), but the game was actually decided by Stockfish hanging its queen to Bg5 (move 28: ...Nxa3?? ignoring the attack, 29.Bxe7 wins the queen outright). Before that, Destroyer had two real tactical near-misses of its own that SF-2200 failed to punish. The win is legitimate (material and position were both correctly exploited after move 29), but it was not surgical — it took 117 plies against an opponent that kept finding second-best moves.

## Critical moments
- **Ply 29, Rad1 (-119cp, best Bc1).** FEN `2r1k2r/p2qbppp/4p3/1p1p3n/2nP1B2/PQN3P1/1P2PPBP/R4RK1 w`. Nh5 attacks Bf4; Rad1 ignores it. gxf4 recaptures materially, but ripping the g-pawn open in front of our own king (already missing its fianchetto shield after Be3-c1-f4 wanderings) is what costs the eval. SF played the passive a6 instead of Nxf4 — we got away with it.
- **Ply 51, Be4 (-112cp, best Ne4).** FEN `2r1rbk1/5ppp/p7/1pqP3n/2n2B1P/P1N3P1/1PQ2PB1/2R2RK1 w`. Vacates g2, the last defender of the long diagonal and king shelter, purely to eye e4/b7 — while Nh5xf4 was already sitting there. Ne4 develops with tempo without self-weakening the king. Same root cause as ply 29: moving/trading the fianchetto bishop without weighing king exposure.
- **Ply 53, b4 (-129cp, best d6).** Pushes a flank pawn while the king is now airy (bishop gone from g2) instead of shoving the connected passer with d6, which gains a tempo on the queen and space. Again king safety and passed-pawn priority both undervalued.
- **Ply 55–57, Bg5→Bxe7 (queen win).** SF blunders (Nxa3 instead of moving the queen); Destroyer finds the only correct follow-up and converts cleanly from here — this, not our own play, is the real turning point.
- **Ply 71, Rd1 (-102cp, best Rxa6).** FEN `3r1k2/5p1p/p2P2p1/1pN4n/1P5P/6P1/5P2/R5K1 w`, completely winning endgame. A free pawn on a6 sat there; instead the rook shuffled to a passive square. Symptomatic of the aimless-move pattern in won endgames that stretched this game to 117 plies instead of ~90.

## Recurring weaknesses
- King safety is not weighed when relocating/trading the fianchetto bishop — twice this game (plies 29, 51) it opened its own king for no compensation.
- In clearly winning endgames, rook/knight shuffle instead of grabbing free material or advancing the most dangerous passer (ply 67 Ra1, ply 71 Rd1 skip Rxa6).
- Missed simple 1-ply "what does the opponent threaten" checks (Nxf4 available twice, unaddressed both times) — only bailed out by SF's own Elo-limited errors.

## What SF2300 would likely do differently
Take the free knight-for-bishop trades when offered (ply 29/51) instead of passing, and would not hang the queen at move 28 — that single blunder is what turned an unclear middlegame into a forced win. Against a cleaner opponent this game is a long, murky draw-ish middlegame, not a rout.

## Ranked action items
1. **King safety (reprioritize up).** Ply 29 and 51 (-119, -112cp) both come from moving/trading the g2 fianchetto bishop without a king-safety penalty for the resulting open diagonal/g-file. This is the single biggest, most repeatable leak this game — bump above check-extensions/qsearch in the queue.
2. **Mop-up/endgame technique (reprioritize up).** Ply 67 (Ra1) and ply 71 (Rd1, -102cp missing free Rxa6) show the engine shuffling instead of grabbing hanging material or pushing the most advanced passer once clearly winning; this is why conversion took 117 plies instead of ~90 and is the direct root cause worth moving ahead of killer/history in priority.
3. **Check extensions + qsearch fix (keep current priority, don't deprioritize).** The two missed Nxf4 threats are exactly the class of shallow tactical blindness this fix targets — SF2300 will not hand these back like SF2200 did.
4. **TT (keep testing as planned).** Deeper effective search should catch both the hanging-bishop threats and the free a6 pawn; no change needed, just confirm it doesn't regress king-safety search depth.
