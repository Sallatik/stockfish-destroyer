# Destroyer 0.7.0 (white) vs SF2500 — win by mate, 101 plies

**Verdict:** Earned, but the longest and least precise of the four. 8...Nd4 (best Na5) let 9.Nxe5! win a clean pawn; Black then bled 90-150cp repeatedly for 30+ moves with no single blunder (8...Nd4 -94, 26...Bh4 -92, 31...a5 -96, 36...Be7 -147, 37...Ke8 -113) while our engine nursed the edge accurately until one real lapse at move 43.

**Our lapse — 43.Bd4 (-489cp, best Rh7+):** already up a full rook, 43.Bd4 centralizes into a square Black can hit with tempo via 43...Bb6!, and 44...Bxd4+ is check along the very diagonal the bishop just sat on. Verified with Stockfish depth 20: Rh7+ (+846) just keeps checking and mates with zero counterplay, versus Bd4/Rxa8/Bxd4+/Rxd4 (+823) which still wins (nets even more material once the smoke clears) but hands Black spite-checks and burns moves. Classic "natural move vs forcing move" gap when already winning big.

**Shorten the game:** beyond the Bd4 lapse, nothing here is wasted motion — converting a one-pawn-then-one-piece edge against a defender that bleeds 90-150cp repeatedly but never outright blunders typically takes exactly this many moves without deeper search. Final position still has 10 men on the board, so tablebases wouldn't have engaged either; the fix for game length in general is the queued search work, not a new heuristic.

**Action items (ranked):**
1. Once eval is decisively winning (say >+600cp) and no forcing tactic is required to stay winning, bias move selection toward checks/forcing moves over natural centralizing or trading moves, specifically to deny the opponent any in-between resource — 43.Bd4 vs Rh7+ is the textbook case. This is a move-selection/eval heuristic, distinct from the queued check-extension search fix.
2. Build a small "don't blow the win" regression suite from spots like this one (43.Bd4) and the 103344 game's 27.Rad1: position + "only the top engine move is acceptable" as the pass condition. Run it after every search/eval change so precision in clearly-winning positions doesn't silently regress.
