## Is this the king-exposure pattern again?
Yes, but not "pawn moves weaken the shield" — there was no self-inflicted shield damage here. The king was forced out of castling by a sound, engine-approved tactic, and the engine then treated an uncastled king with queens on the board as a non-issue, repeatedly choosing material grabs/trades (Bxe6, Ng5xe6, Qxb4) over anything that tucks the king in or activates Rh1/Bc1 — Rh1 first moves at move 20 (capturing, not connecting), Bc1 first moves at move 32. Root cause is undervaluing "uncastled + undeveloped rook/bishop with queens on," not pawn-shield holes.

## What SF2400 did well
Correctly read that 11...Bxf2+ works (Qb6 makes Kxf2 illegal), then never let the king settle: a long chain of checks (Rxd6+, Qxg2+, Re6+, Qe2+, Rc6+, Qg4+, Re2+, Qe4+) each gained tempo while dragging the king further from cover — textbook technique once an opponent misses castling. It let the forced mate slip twice (Black's moves 28 and 32) but never risked the point since the material/king-safety gap was already decisive.
