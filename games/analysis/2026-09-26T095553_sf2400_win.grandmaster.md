**Verdict:** A long grind. We built a +1.7 opening edge (Sicilian, ...Qb6) then gave large chunks of it back four separate times through passive play — most strikingly, dxc5 was the objectively best move on three consecutive moves (6.Rb1, 7.Bd3, 8.h3) and we played something else each time, bleeding +1.7 down to +0.2 before move 9. Later slips (27.Nd4, 35.Bf1, 41.a7) each gave back 1.2-1.7 pawns to a more active alternative. SF2400 matched or exceeded every one of these (worst: 41...Rxf2?? instead of Bxf2+, -3.25, right after our own a7 slip), so the advantage never seriously changed hands. The apparent disaster at move 56 (c7, logged as a -86.87 "mistake") is not real — see below. Earned: yes, but far less efficiently than the position deserved; this should have ended 20-30 moves sooner.

**Critical moments:**
- Moves 6-8 (Rb1/Bd3/h3, missing dxc5 all three times): the clearest, most reproducible pattern in this game — repeated refusal to resolve central tension in our favor.
- 41.a7 (best Ra7+, -1.74) / 41...Rxf2?? (best Bxf2+, -3.25): a mutual howler in the sharpest moment of the game; Black's error is nearly double the size of ours, so we come out further ahead than before the exchange.
- 56.c7 "blown mate" (eval logged 9987→1300): verified independently at depth 24 — the resulting position is a genuine forced mate (mate in 6-9 depending on depth); the depth-18 grading search just didn't prove it at that instant (it also showed a plain cp score at depth 16, then a mate score again at 17-18, in the same run). This is a scoring-harness artifact near mate scores, not an engine error; the position never stopped being completely won and mate followed 5 moves later regardless.

**What SF2500+ punishes:** the repeated missed dxc5 and the later passive retreats (27.Nd4, 35.Bf1). A 2500 does not blunder back with 41...Rxf2, so those 1.2-1.7 pawn giveaways would stand and could turn a routine win into a real fight.
