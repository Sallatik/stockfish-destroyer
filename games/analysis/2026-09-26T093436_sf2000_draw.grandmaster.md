# Grandmaster Analysis: 2026-09-26T093436_sf2000_draw (Destroyer 0.5.0 vs SF 2000, draw by repetition)

## Verdict
White built a clean +150 to +170cp edge through moves 14-18 on Black's slow opening (repeated delays of ...e6), then self-destructed starting move 21 with a g-pawn capture that opened its own king, followed by three straight captures instead of one consolidating bishop move, handing Black a near-winning attack. White was objectively lost (-480 to -680cp) by the finish, and the threefold repetition (moves 40-44) was a lucky escape, not a self-inflicted draw from a winning position. This should have been a clean win — instead it twice came within one accurate Black move of being a loss.

Qxa4 alone loses 488cp — the queen leaves for a4 while Black's queen, knight and bishop swarm the king; only Black's second-best Rxh3 (Qg7+ was crushing) kept White alive. Visible material beat an eval with no price on king safety, three times in a row.

This is the real losing move: the second time Black handed White a lifeline (also move 23, Kf8 instead of the crushing Qxf7) and White immediately threw it away instead of consolidating.
- **Moves 40-44, the repetition** — FEN `4r3/6k1/2P1p3/3pP3/3P2n1/P2Q1Pq1/1P2R1B1/4bK2 w - - 3 42`. This is a save, not the "repeat when winning" bug: Black, Elo-limited, missed Nh2+ and other wins and let the draw happen. Don't lump it in with the earlier repetition problem.

## Recurring weaknesses
- Own-king pawn breaks: a g-pawn capture opening lines to our own king, 3 for 3 recent games now.
- Material greed over king safety: a free capture is chosen over a quiet consolidating move (Bg4 declined three plies running) even as the opponent builds a mating attack.
- Can't bank a gift: twice this game Black handed back a near-equal position and White immediately blundered again instead of holding what it had.

Use move 21 of this game as the regression test — the fix should prefer Kg2 or holding tension over gxh5.
