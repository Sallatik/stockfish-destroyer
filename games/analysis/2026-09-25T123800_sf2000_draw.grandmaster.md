## Verdict
This game was objectively lost, not drawn on merit. By move 24 material-only eval (no PST) had already thrown White into a -900 to -1300cp hole via passive opening play. The draw came from two lucky breaks that are new problems beyond the known opening/PST gap: (1) SF2000 twice failed to execute forced mates our king walks handed it, and (2) in the R+N vs Q ending our engine's aimless rook shuffling (Ra2/Rg2+/Re2+) accidentally produced a threefold repetition that SF2000, also Elo-capped in technique, never broke.

## Critical moments — king walks into mating nets
- **Ply 47, Ke2** (FEN `1b1r2k1/pp2rpp1/5q2/5b1p/P7/B1PnP1P1/6QP/RN2K1NR w KQ - 0 24`). In check from Nxd3+, we played Ke2 (SF eval: forced mate, -9988) instead of Kd2 (only -979). Material-only eval scores Ke2 and Kd2 *identically* — neither move gains/loses material — so the choice between "walks into the Bf5/Qf6/Rd8 crossfire" and "steps toward safety" was effectively a coin flip. This is not a PST-fixable bug; it's a missing king-danger term.
- **Ply 113, Kf3** (FEN `8/p4p1k/P6P/1p4p1/2b5/6R1/4KN1P/2q5 w - - 0 57`). In check from Bxc4+, we walked Kf3 into a mating net (Qf4+ etc., -9982) instead of blocking with Rd3 (-1327, merely lost). Same pattern: king-in-check responses (block vs. flee) are scored as equal by material alone.
- **Why SF2000 missed both mates**: at ply 48 it played Bd6 instead of the mating Rxe3+ (eval collapsed from -9988 to -831); at ply 124 it played Bd5+ instead of continuing the mating sequence after Qf4+ (from -9984 to -1115). Elo-limited SF reliably finds "just keep winning" moves but drops long forced-mate lines requiring several only-moves. Exploitable: when clearly lost, our best practical try is to keep the king in the middle of maximum complications (checks, multiple attackers) rather than simplify — SF2000 converts simple technical wins far more reliably than deep forced mates.
- **Plies ~180-207, repetition drift**: down a queen for rook+knight, our engine shuffled Ra2 ↔ Rg2+ ↔ Re2+ purely because these looked "safe" under material eval, stumbling into a triggered threefold. SF2000 (eval consistently +650 to +1085 for it) never found the technique to make progress and avoid the repeat — this was an accidental save, not engine skill.

## Recurring weaknesses (new, beyond opening/PST/repetition-detection gaps already queued)
- No king-danger evaluation: king-in-check replies and voluntary king moves are chosen with zero regard for attacker count/open lines when material is tied, causing literal coin-flip walks into mate.
- No distinction between "block a check" and "move the king" when both are material-neutral — search/move-ordering has no bias toward reducing checks/keeping the king shielded.
- No sense of "seek complications when lost" vs. "simplify when winning" — the engine plays the same way regardless of whether it's -1300 or +100.

## Ranked concrete action items
1. **Add a king-danger eval term (independent of PST king-safety)**: penalize positions where own king sits on an open file/diagonal facing enemy queen+rook+minor, scaled by attacker count and king's distance from back rank/corner. This directly targets the Ke2/Kf3 coin-flip blunders, since PST alone won't distinguish these two mate-losing moves from safe alternatives when material and even generic king-PST value are close.
2. **When in check, bias move ordering/eval toward block/capture over king flight** when both are otherwise eval-neutral — add a small bonus for interposing pieces vs. moving the king into open board, to break the "equal-eval" ties that caused both blunders here.
3. **Add a "complications when losing" heuristic**: once eval is clearly negative (e.g., < -300cp), prefer keeping more pieces on the board and creating multi-piece threats/checks near the enemy king over simplifying trades — SF at capped Elo drops long forced sequences far more often than short technical wins, so messier lost positions convert to draws/saves more often.
4. **Verify the v3 repetition/contempt logic actively seeks repetition (not just detects it) when eval is strongly negative** (e.g., < -300cp), not only avoids it when ahead. This game only drew because the shuffle accidentally repeated; a directed "steer toward the repeating position" search bias in clearly-lost endgames would make this a reproducible defensive resource instead of luck.
