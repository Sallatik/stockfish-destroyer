# Grandmaster Analysis — 2026-09-26T091234_sf2100_win (Destroyer 0.4.0 vs SF 2100, W, mate in 28)

## Verdict
Won mostly on SF's mistakes, not our technique. 6.Nd5!? was a sound provocation, but the game only turned decisively winning because Black blundered twice in a row (12...Nf6, 13...Kd7). We then converted cleanly to mate; our one flagged inaccuracy (23.Qc3) happened in an already +6-pawn position and changed nothing.

## Critical moments
1. **6.Nd5!? (ours)** — good practical try vs the ...Bg4/...g6 setup; keeps Black's structure and king under long-term pressure. No complaint, a 2200 plays this.
2. **11.c3 (ours, -95cp untagged)** — engine's Ne3 was better: keeps the knight flexible instead of committing the pawn structure and opening the c-file before it's needed. Cost nothing here since Black's own reply was also loose, but it's a pattern (see below).
3. **12...Nf6 (SF, -275cp, real blunder)** — after 11...exd5 12.cxd4, Black had to play Bxd4 to stay level. Nf6 ignores the hanging d5 pawn and the newly-opened e-file (Re1 vs Ke8), letting 13.exd5+ win a pawn with a discovered check.
4. **13...Kd7 (SF, -253cp, the losing move)** — Kf8 keeps the king relatively safe and the position merely worse; Kd7 walks into dxc5+/c6 (a monster passed/cramping pawn) and a permanent king hunt that runs to mate on move 28. This is the actual decisive moment of the game, and it's entirely Black's error, not our engineering.
5. **23.Qc3 (ours, -112cp, our only flagged mistake)** — Bd2 coordinates the bishop pair better toward the black king; Qc3 lets ...Rb6 in. Irrelevant at +630 vs +518.
6. **26.Qa5 (ours, missed forced mate, Bxg5 was faster)** — cosmetic only; position was already +12 (dead won).

## Recurring weaknesses
- Mechanical pawn-lever timing: commits structure (c3) before checking whether a piece move (Ne3) keeps more options — a positional/mobility judgment gap, not a tactical one.
- Doesn't reliably find the fastest mate once totally winning (Qa5 vs Bxg5) — consistent with shallow search rather than an eval bug; harmless here but will matter for adjudication/time pressure in tougher games.

## Was the win earned, and does this change the plan queue?
Largely earned by opponent error, not by us exploiting a specific weakness beyond what's already queued. No reordering needed — TT and check-extensions/qsearch (already at the top of the queue) are exactly what would fix both symptoms seen here (imprecise c3-vs-Ne3 choice, slow mate-finding).

2. **Check extensions + qsearch fixes** (already queued): the winning tactic here was a discovered check down an open e-file; better search on checks improves both finding such shots and finding forced mates quickly (Bxg5 vs Qa5) once winning.
3. **King safety / open-file-to-king term** (already queued): would make the engine actively steer toward exposing the enemy king (as happened here almost by accident) rather than only cashing in when the opponent walks into it themselves.
