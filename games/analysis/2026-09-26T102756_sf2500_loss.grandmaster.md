No single blunder — a strategic bleed. White had a comfortable opening (+1.04 by move 8), created a permanent target with 22.b4?! and mishandled it with 29.Rdc2 (the real hinge, -1.19→-2.25), let Black build connected passed c/d-pawns, then spent moves 39-42 chasing flank pawns with a rook instead of blockading them, and moves 54-55 pushing pawns instead of activating the king — the same "walk the king" instruction was the engine's own listed best move four separate times across the game (31, 33, 54, 55) and was never played. ## What SF2500 did well
Textbook rook-behind-the-passed-pawn (Rd6) plus a rook parked on the queening file (Rc1); king marched c8-c7-c6...b4-b2 to personally escort its pawns while White's sat on g1/h2 through the whole rook ending; wasn't flawless (occasional 60-140cp slips) but never let a winning position slip.

## Recurring weaknesses
- No rook/passed-pawn blockade logic: the rook leaves the controlling file/square to win material elsewhere (plies 79-83).
- No endgame king-activity term: "walk the king toward the action" was the engine's own listed best move four separate times (plies 61, 65, 107, 109) and it was never played.
- Commits to structural pawn weaknesses (22.b4) with no follow-up plan once the target is fixed.

## Queue impact
This game is a direct case study for the already-queued "pawn structure/passed pawns/mop-up" item — no new item needed, but it should move ahead of PVS+LMR+aspiration, and within it the king-activity/mop-up half deserves at least equal weight to the passed-pawn-bonus half: 4 of this game's 8 flagged mistakes are "pushed a pawn/rook instead of walking the king."

## Ranked action items
1. **[Highest impact] Implement the mop-up/king-activity term first, inside the queued passed-pawn work**: reward king distance to own passed pawns and to the enemy king, scaling up as material drops.
