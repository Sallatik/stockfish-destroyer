# Grandmaster Analysis: 2026-09-25T124045_sf1800_win (Destroyer 0.3.0 vs SF1800, 1-0, mate)

## Verdict
Won on the back of a large, correctly-earned middlegame edge (Bxa4+ won a pawn and permanently exiled Black's king to the center), but our own two real mistakes (c4?! and Qc5+??) gave most of that edge away for free around move 20-25. The win was ultimately **gifted back**: SF1800 handed us a full point of equity with Be8 (should be Bxc6, move 52) right when our position was at its worst (-342). Without that blunder this game is drawish or worse. Endgame conversion afterward was competent but slow and padded with more SF1800 errors (Ke6 move 29, Bd7 move 30, Bb6 move 45 hanging material).

## Critical moments
1. **Ply 39, c4?! (best: h4)** — FEN `r2r4/2qbkp2/2p1nbpp/1p1Bp3/4P3/2P1NN2/PPQ2PPP/3R1RK1 w - - 6 20`. We were +134 with total bind on a king stuck in the center. c4 opens the center and walks into ...Nd4!, a fork/central break that liquidates into equality. h4 (or any quiet consolidation, e.g. Rd2/Kh1) keeps the space edge and the bad black king with no counterplay. This is a one-move tactical oversight (missing a knight fork), not a strategic error — classic symptom of shallow/unreliable search.
2. **Ply 45, Qc5+?? (best: fxe3)** — FEN `r2r4/2qbkp2/2p2bpp/1P1B4/4P3/4p3/PP3PPP/3R1RK1 w - - 0 23`. Black's e3 pawn (two ranks deep in our camp, next to our king) is the single most dangerous feature on the board and must be eliminated immediately. Instead we play a "free" check that trades queens but leaves e3 alive; two moves later we're still cleaning it up while Black's king reaches d6/e7 and activates. Eval swings from 0 to -240. A human would reflexively take fxe3 here — a loose, advanced enemy pawn near your own king outranks a check that achieves nothing concrete.
3. **Ply 49, Bxc6+ (best: Bxf7+)** — minor but same pattern: grabbing the more "available" pawn instead of the more damaging one (f7 rips open Black's kingside further and keeps initiative alive); only a 23cp cost here but consistent with move-choice-by-availability rather than move-choice-by-consequence.
4. **Ply 52, Black's Be8 (best Bxc6)** — this is the game. Black was up material and structurally fine (-317, i.e. +3 for Black) and simply misses that Bxc6 keeps the extra pawn; Be8 lets us grab a8 for free and the game flips back to near-equal (+30). This is SF1800's error, not ours — flag it as evidence the win required opponent cooperation.
5. **Moves 55-90 endgame** — technically sound (two connected passers pushed to promotion, rook activity fine) but slow: repeated non-progress moves (Rd3, Rb3, h4, g3, Kg2, hxg5) each costing small eval (10-70cp against best) while never risking the win only because SF1800 kept reciprocating with its own drops (Ke6 -248, Bd7 -139, g4 -52, Bb6 -92, the last one hanging the exchange outright).

## Recurring weaknesses
- Missing short tactical shots one/two plies deep before committing to central pawn breaks (c4→Nd4).
- No sense of "urgency" for removing a dangerous, deeply advanced enemy pawn versus playing a flashy check — pure eval-function blind spot, not a search problem.
- Picks the more available capture over the more consequential one (Bxc6+ vs Bxf7+) — again an eval-function issue, not search.
- Winning endgames are converted but inefficiently, burning moves on non-critical improving moves rather than the most direct plan; harmless here only because the opponent is also error-prone at Elo 1800.

## Priority notes on the existing queue (no new items re-proposed)

- The Qc5+ blunder is a pure eval-function gap (undervaluing a dangerous advanced enemy pawn near the king), which should bump **king-safety/passed-pawn eval work** to explicitly include *opponent* pawns advanced into our own territory, not just our own passers — this is currently framed one-sidedly in the queue description.
- Bottom line: **this win would very likely be a draw or loss against SF2200**, since a 2200-strength opponent does not hand back 287cp for free the way SF1800 did at move 52 — the two real mistakes (134cp, 240cp) would stand uncorrected.
